# Hi, I'm Ali 👋

I'm a **Physics PhD researcher at the University of Pittsburgh** working at the intersection of **statistical inference, data analysis, simulation, and scientific computing**.

My research focuses on extracting weak cosmological signals from large observational datasets, with experience building end-to-end analysis pipelines involving **large-scale data processing, numerical methods, statistical estimation, simulations, and high-performance computing**.

I'm currently interested in applying these skills to problems in **data science, quantitative modeling, scientific computing, and computational physics**.

## Featured Projects

### 🔭 [Moving Lens Analysis Pipeline](https://github.com/ali-beheshti/moving-lens-analysis-pipeline)

An end-to-end scientific analysis pipeline for measuring the **moving lens effect** using CMB maps and galaxy catalogs.

**What it involves:**  
`Statistical inference` · `Signal processing` · `Large datasets` · `Simulations` · `HPC` · `Python`

The project includes matched filtering, catalog–map analysis, pairwise estimators, covariance estimation, null tests, and validation on cosmological simulations.

## Research

My PhD research uses observations of the **Cosmic Microwave Background** and **large-scale structure** to study the motion and matter distribution of galaxies and dark matter halos.

A major focus of my work has been developing and validating methods for detecting the **moving lens effect** — a very weak CMB signal generated by the transverse motion of gravitational lenses.

📄 **Publication:**  
*Moving lens effect: Simulations, forecasts, and foreground mitigation*  
Physical Review D **111**, 043510 (2025)
